How repeat purchases were traced

For each of the 68 additional wallets, the evidence connects a SHYFT sale to a SOL funding transfer and then a SHYFT purchase. All three transactions occur in the same slot, in that order; 63 chains use consecutive transaction indices. All subsequent identified SHYFT trades by these wallets are included.

Where withdrawn SOL went

The audit traced 107 major exits through 10,700 intermediate addresses and 10,807 transfers. In the archived histories, no SHYFT balance movements were found at those intermediate addresses after receipt.

Of the traced routes, 67 delivered 256.188660830 SOL to collector BB8bMKc4FcmNWwbSvykwqd8WFNHMmsWiMBneTDue7AEj; 40 delivered 37.993483858 SOL to the funder. These are gross capital flows, not profit.

The collector made two separate 70 SOL transfers, as well as transfers of 56.115 SOL and 4 SOL. A transfer of 70 SOL does not establish a 70 SOL trading result. Separately, 127 SOL sent from the funder to three addresses led to USDC purchases; no SHYFT purchase was identified there. Converting SOL to USDC is not a SHYFT buyback expense.

Tracing stops at the specified group, funder and collector boundaries, whose activity was examined separately. Mixed funds and unrelated activity beyond those boundaries cannot all be assigned to this operation.

Inventory and limits of the conclusion

The 98 wallets retain 106,843,602.069960 SHYFT across 38 positive balances. This inventory is unvalued in the cash result; its purchase cost has already been deducted.

A further 67,003,134.999999 SHYFT went to six external recipients. Their common control is unproven, so their downstream results are excluded. Their identified swaps changed assets by +6.461868808 SOL and +3,022.411308 USDC; those figures are not a fully allocated recipient PnL.

This is the 23 October 2026 historical snapshot, not live monitoring. Scoped address histories were checked through finalized slot 460222992. Token balances and external-recipient balances were captured at the separate slots documented in the review packet. Later activity, unidentified wallets, external expenses or contractual profit-sharing may change a broader accounting result.

The reported repayment through a centralized exchange and the user’s agreed share are not established by this on-chain evidence. The conclusion is limited: the identified trades and traced repeat purchases do not reconcile to 70 SOL.
